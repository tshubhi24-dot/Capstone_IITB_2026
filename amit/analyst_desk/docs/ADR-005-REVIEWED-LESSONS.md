# Reviewed development lessons

## Problem and decision

Research can repeat mistakes found by the critic or corrected by an analyst. Add an explicit improvement loop: run a registered public development question, score its output with existing code checks, reflect on an actual failed check or analyst edit, then present a bounded lesson proposal for human review. Store accepted and rejected decisions, with reasons, in the local LangGraph store.

Ordinary research remains production work. Existing runs keep their original classification. Only new requests matching the bundled public development question and scope receive development admission. Expected answers, protected questions and evaluator labels are not copied into the application. Development runs stop at note review and cannot publish.

## Review and reuse

A proposal binds its originating run, event and output versions. Accept, edit and reject operate on the exact proposal hash. An edit creates a new proposal requiring its own decision. No proposal is automatically accepted. Acceptance does not silently change an existing playbook: the analyst explicitly freezes a version containing accepted lessons.

New research may opt into one frozen playbook. Retrieve at most three lessons relevant to the selected company/year scope and processing stage. Reusable guidance comes from a small code-owned catalogue: align fiscal periods, verify current facts, preserve risk qualifiers and cite exact evidence. Proposal text, previous questions and reviewer reasons remain local. Advice cannot replace current SEC evidence or relax checks, permissions, spending limits or human approvals. The critic receives no lesson guidance.

Playbook identity and selected lesson versions appear in the research plan and final note payload. Revalidate before continuation, tool/model calls, review and publication; publication repeats the check in its existing transaction. Revoking a lesson invalidates pending work using it. Lessons have a separate off/on switch so a later E3 experiment can hold ordinary analyst memory constant.

## Verification and remaining human evidence

Use fictional fixtures to test exact-version decisions, rejected-lesson exclusion, applicability, frozen versions, revocation races, provider-context boundaries and persistence. Verify Docker using a separate operator namespace without changing analyst records, submitting human decisions or making paid model requests.

Real accepted/rejected decisions and held-out improvement remain pending until a person reviews genuine development-derived proposals and an independent evaluator runs the frozen experiment. A test fixture is not evidence of human review or research improvement. Code-owned reflection supports code-decidable failure categories; semantic applicability and counterexamples need human assessment.
